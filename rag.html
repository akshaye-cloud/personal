💡 Key Takeaways

  • RAG does not send the entire knowledge base to the LLM.
  • Documents are split into smaller chunks.
  • Chunks become vector embeddings.
  • The vector database stores vectors + text + metadata.
  • The question is also converted into a vector.
  • Only relevant chunks are retrieved.
  • The LLM generates using the retrieved context.

🎯 Why RAG Matters

  • Works with very large knowledge bases.
  • Reduces the amount of context sent to the LLM.
  • Can provide answers grounded in company data.
  • Knowledge can be updated without retraining the LLM.
  • Can combine semantic search with databases.

🧠 Important Distinction

Vector search is not the same as a database query.

"What is Apple's revenue?" may be better answered with structured financial data.

"Why did Apple's revenue growth slow?" may require retrieving reports, commentary and analysis.

Your mental model should be:

Don't give the LLM the entire library. Find the right books/pages first, then give the relevant pages to the LLM.

Library analogy

Bloomberg = enormous library
Documents = books
Chunks = pages/sections
Embeddings = numerical fingerprints of meaning
Vector database = searchable map of those fingerprints
User question = "What am I looking for?"
Retrieval = librarian finding relevant pages
Augmentation = putting those pages on the desk
LLM = expert reading those pages and explaining them
